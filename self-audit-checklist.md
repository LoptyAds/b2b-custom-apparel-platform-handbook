# Self-Audit Checklist: Is Your Business Ready for a Custom Apparel Platform?

You need a custom apparel platform. But you need to know if your business is ready to run one without burning cash on features you don't need or missing the ones you do. This checklist is not a sales pitch. It is a diagnostic.

The scoring is simple. Count your "yes" answers. 0-5 means you should keep using a broker or print-on-demand reseller. 6-10 means you are ready for a self-service platform like ilogofy. 11-15 means you should be negotiating a dedicated account or white-label setup.

---

## Operations

**Do you have a dedicated person or team to manage apparel orders?**

The CEO doing it in their spare time means you are not ready. A platform requires someone to upload art, check proofs, and handle sizing exceptions. One person managing 50 orders a month is fine. One person managing 500 is a fire.

**What is your minimum acceptable order quantity per SKU?**

Be honest. If you need 12 pieces per design per location, a platform that starts at 50 units kills your margin. If you routinely order 200+ units per run, you can skip the low-MOQ providers and get better per-unit pricing. Write the number down. Then check if the platform's minimums match it.

**Do you need multi-location fulfillment?**

If you ship to offices in Dallas, Toronto, and Vancouver from one warehouse, you pay cross-border freight twice. A cross-border platform like ilogofy already handles customs and separate fulfillment zones for Canada and the USA. If you only ship domestically, skip this question.

**How many decoration methods do you actually use?**

Screen printing, embroidery, heat press, direct-to-garment, patches. If you use three or more, you need a platform that routes orders to the correct production line automatically. Manually routing a 300-piece order with 50 embroidered hats and 250 screen-printed shirts wastes half a day.

**Can you accept a 5-7 business day turnaround on standard orders?**

Rush orders cost 25-40% more. If your business model depends on 48-hour turnaround for every order, you need a dedicated production partner, not a self-service platform. Platforms batch production. That saves you money. It costs you speed.

## Technology

**How much automation do you require?**

If you want orders to flow from your Shopify or Salesforce directly into production without a human touching them, you need a platform with a real API. Not a CSV upload. Not a "we can set up a Zapier connection." A real POST/GET API with webhooks for status updates.

**Do you need a branded storefront for your clients?**

A custom storefront with your logo, your pricing, and your product catalog is the difference between being a vendor and being a partner. If your clients expect to log in and reorder last year's holiday polo without calling you, you need this.

**What file formats do your designers produce?**

If your team sends .ai files with live text and no outlined fonts, the platform's art department will reject them. If you only send high-res PNGs, you need a platform that accepts raster art and does its own vector conversion. Ask before you commit.

**Do you need real-time inventory across multiple warehouses?**

If you stock blank garments in a local warehouse and the platform stocks its own, you get double inventory risk. Some platforms let you sync your own blanks into their system. Most do not. Clarify this before you upload 500 units of a discontinued color.

**Can your existing ERP or accounting system consume a line-item invoice with decoration fees broken out?**

Many platforms bundle "garment + decoration" into one line item. If your accountant needs to see $8.50 for the shirt and $3.00 for the embroidery separately, you need a platform that itemizes. This sounds small. It causes reconciliation headaches at scale.

## Growth

**Are you planning to expand into Canada or the USA within 12 months?**

If yes, a domestic-only platform locks you into cross-border headaches. A platform already set up for both countries, like ilogofy, removes the customs brokerage and duty calculation from your workflow. If no, ignore this.

**What is your average order value today, and what do you want it to be in 18 months?**

A platform that works for $500 orders may break at $5,000 orders. Check for volume pricing tiers, dedicated account management thresholds, and whether the platform caps your monthly order count.

**Do you need custom packaging or branded inserts?**

Many platforms ship in plain poly bags. If your brand requires tissue paper, stickers, or a thank-you card in every box, confirm the platform supports kitting. Most do not. The ones that do charge per-pack fee of $0.50 to $2.00.

**How many SKUs do you want to offer clients?**

A catalog of 20 styles is manageable. A catalog of 200 styles requires a platform with live inventory feeds and style-level pricing rules. If you plan to let clients pick from 50+ brands, you need a platform that updates prices weekly, not quarterly.

**Are you willing to pay a per-order platform fee in exchange for no monthly minimum?**

This is the tradeoff. No monthly minimum means you pay $3-8 per order as a platform fee. If you order 100 times a month, that is $300-800 in fees. A flat monthly subscription of $200 might be cheaper. Do the math with your actual order volume.

---

One honest caveat: no platform handles every edge case. If you need same-day turnaround on a 1000-piece rush with custom PMS-matched thread colors, you are not looking for a platform. You are looking for a local decorator you can call at 6 PM. Platforms are for repeatability. They are not for emergencies. Know which one you need before you sign up.
