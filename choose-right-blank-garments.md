# How to Choose the Right Blank Garments for Corporate Apparel

The garment blank is the foundation. Pick a bad one, and your embroidery floats, your screen print cracks, and the whole thing looks cheap regardless of logo quality. Fit, fabric, and weight aren't style choices here, they're mechanical constraints that determine how decoration sticks, how long the shirt lasts, and whether it actually hangs right on a person.

## Fabric Types and Decoration Realities

**100% cotton** is what screen printers want. The fibers grip plastisol ink without bleeding, fine lines stay sharp. But cotton shrinks. A Gildan 5000 tee can lose 5% of its length after one hot wash, if you're placing a front-chest logo at 8 inches from the collar, that logo ends up at 7.2 inches after laundry day. Cotton also wrinkles visibly. For a corporate team wearing these in client meetings, that matters.

**Polyester** doesn't shrink, dries fast, holds color wash after wash. But heat is the enemy. Screen print a standard ink on polyester and the fabric dye migrates into the ink, white areas turn pink or yellow within weeks. You need low-bleed inks or a poly-specific system. Embroidery works fine, but drop the thread tension; polyester has less stretch than cotton and will pucker around dense stitch patterns.

**Blends (50/50 or 65/35 cotton/poly)** are compromises. They shrink less than cotton, breathe better than poly, and accept screen ink decently if poly content stays under 50%. But they feel like what they are: workhorse fabrics. Construction crews and warehouse staff wear these because durability beats hand feel.

**Tri-blends (cotton, polyester, rayon)** show up more in corporate catalogs now. They drape softer, pill slower, feel premium. But rayon causes ink adhesion problems. If you're printing a large solid fill on a tri-blend hoodie, expect a higher reject rate. I've seen shops lose 15% of a run on this.

## Weight: Not Just a Number

GSM (grams per square meter) or ounces per square yard. Corporate polos and tees live between 140 and 220 GSM.

**140-160 GSM** works for summer giveaways and trade show shirts you hand out once. But lightweight fabric shifts under the embroidery hoop. Registration drifts on multi-color logos. Dense stitch patterns pucker. If you're doing a full back or a left-chest design over 5,000 stitches, go heavier.

**170-190 GSM** is where most corporate orders land. Port Authority's K420 polo (180 GSM) handles embroidery cleanly, the collar holds shape, the fabric doesn't sag after washing. Most B2B platforms, including ilogofy's catalog, stock midweight as default because it works for both screen printing and embroidery without separate prep.

**200+ GSM** is for hoodies, work jackets, premium tees. Holds embroidery beautifully. But these take longer to dry after washing. If you're running 500 pieces and need same-day shipping, that drying time is a bottleneck.

## Fit: The Problem Nobody Talks About

Unisex cuts are men's cuts with a boxier shape. They fit about 70% of bodies, but the shoulders are wider and the waist is looser than most women's frames. A ladies cut has narrower shoulders, higher armholes, tapered waist. This isn't about gender politics, it's about whether your logo sits flat or gets distorted by fabric pulling across the bust.

Here's the real issue: most corporate orders default to unisex because it simplifies inventory. But if your team is over 40% women, you will hear complaints. Some platforms now let you split an order between unisex and ladies cuts within the same catalog entry, one purchase order, two fits. Check for that before you commit to a supplier.

## Brands I've Actually Used

**Gildan 5000** (heavy cotton tee, 203 GSM). Cheap, consistent, default for promo runs under 100 pieces. The collar warps after 10 washes. For a one-day event shirt, nobody cares.

**Hanes Beefy-T** (6.1 oz, 207 GSM). Better collar than Gildan, slightly softer. Common in hospitality uniforms that get worn weekly.

**Port Authority K420** (pique knit polo, 180 GSM). Industry standard for corporate polos. The pique texture hides minor embroidery mistakes. Collar buttons are reinforced. 40+ colors. If you're ordering custom polos and don't have a strong opinion on fabric, this is the safe bet.

**Bella+Canvas 3001** (tri-blend tee, 150 GSM). Lightweight, soft, modern cut. Tech companies and startups love these. But the thin fabric means embroidery backing is mandatory. High-density stitch counts will show through.

One thing nobody tells you: brand consistency shifts every 18-24 months. Gildan's 5000 series from 2022 had a different weave than the 2024 run. Some screen printers reported ink absorption issues. Always order a pre-production sample of the actual blank you plan to use, not just the brand name.
