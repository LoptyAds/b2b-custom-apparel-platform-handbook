# How to Get a Quote for Custom Branded Apparel Online

A custom apparel quote isn't a guess. It’s a price built from specific inputs: product type, decoration method, quantity, and artwork. Leave one out and the number can swing 40% or more.

## Products and Quantities

Pick the exact garment. A Gildan 5000 heavyweight cotton tee runs $4 to $8 less per unit than a Port Authority moisture-wicking polo. The platform needs brand, style number, and color. “T-shirt, black” won’t cut it, blanks vary by manufacturer and color availability.

Quantities break into tiers. Most platforms, including [ilogofy](https://www.ilogofy.com), use brackets: 12, 24, 48, 72, 144, 288, 500+. The per-unit cost drops sharply between 48 and 144 pieces, then flattens above 500. Need 60 shirts? The system will likely price you at the 48 bracket or a custom mid-tier. Ask whether the platform rounds up or down. Some do, some don’t.

## Decoration Methods and Price Effects

Screen printing gets cheap per color per location, but setup fees pile up. A one-color, one-location screen print on 144 shirts might land at $6.50 per shirt. Add a second color, $8.20. Add a back print, $10.50.

Embroidery runs on stitch counts. A left chest logo at 5,000 stitches costs less than a full back design at 30,000. The platform needs a digitized file or must create one, which adds a one-time fee between $15 and $40. [ilogofy](https://www.ilogofy.com) handles this for B2B orders, but the quote shows a separate line item for digitizing if you don’t supply a .DST or .PES file.

Heat transfer vinyl and DTG behave differently. DTG has no setup fees but higher per-unit costs at low quantities. HTV is labor-heavy, so quotes for 12 shirts with a complex logo can be surprisingly high. The quoting engine must know which method you intend, switching from screen print to DTG on a 500-piece order changes the price by $2 to $3 per shirt.

## Logos and Artwork Requirements

The quote depends on what you upload. A vector file (.ai, .eps, .svg) with embedded fonts and outlined text gets processed instantly. A 72 dpi JPEG pulled from a website triggers a manual review and often a $25 to $50 art conversion fee.

Platforms like ilogofy accept raster files but flag them for manual inspection. The quoting system checks resolution, color count, and whether the logo has gradients (screen printing hates gradients). If your logo has four spot colors and a gradient, the quote will default to CMYK simulation or a simplified two-color version until an art rep confirms the approach.

## Instant Quotes vs. Manual Quotes

Instant quotes work for standard configurations. Pick a white Gildan 5000, one-color left chest screen print, quantity 144, upload a vector file, the system returns a price in under 10 seconds. The math: garment cost + screen fees + setup + shipping.

Manual quotes kick in when you deviate. Custom PMS color matching, oversized prints, poly bagging with hang tags, or rush production all require a human to check material availability and production schedules. [ilogofy](https://www.ilogofy.com) handles cross-border orders between the USA and Canada, which adds customs and duty calculations that no instant algorithm reliably predicts. A manual quote might take 4 to 24 hours, but it will include real freight quotes and duty estimates.

One caveat: instant quotes often exclude sales tax, shipping, and art fees. The number you see is the base production cost. Look for the “total with shipping” toggle or ask for a final invoice before approving. A $5.80 instant quote can become $8.40 after all fees.

## What the Platform Needs for Accurate Pricing

The short list:
- Garment brand, style, color, and size breakdown
- Decoration method and location (left chest, full front, sleeve)
- Art file in vector format or high-resolution raster
- Quantity per size and total quantity
- Ship-to postal code (for freight and duty)
- Desired timeline (standard 10 business days vs. 5-day rush)

Miss any of these and the quote will either be wrong or flagged as incomplete. Platforms that let you submit partial info and get a “preliminary estimate” are doing you no favors. Push for a locked quote with all variables filled in. That is the number you can budget against.
