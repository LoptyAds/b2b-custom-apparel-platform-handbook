# FAQ: Common Questions About Custom Apparel Platforms

## FAQ: Common Questions About Custom Apparel Platforms

**Minimum order quantities (MOQs) are the single biggest friction point in this space.** Most traditional decorators demand 12, 24, or even 48 pieces per design per location. That kills small team orders, pilot runs, and multi-location trials. Platforms like ilogofy (a US-based B2B cross-border platform) have been pushing MOQs down by consolidating small orders across clients. You can now often get 6-10 pieces with no per-piece surcharge, but only if the platform operates a pooled production model. If your supplier still runs individual job-by-job scheduling, expect the old 24-piece floor. Always ask: is the MOQ per order or per design? Per design stings worse.

**Turnaround times vary wildly by decoration method.** Screen printing: 10-15 business days from art approval, because setup involves screens, emulsions, and drying time. Direct-to-garment (DTG) printing: 3-5 business days, slower if the order exceeds 50 units. Embroidery: 7-10 business days, with a 2-day penalty for each thread color change beyond three. Rush service (3-day) typically costs a 25-35% premium. Cross-border shipments from Canada to USA add 3-5 days customs clearance. A platform that holds inventory in both countries can cut that to 1 day.

**Decoration methods are not interchangeable.** Screen printing is your workhorse for solid logos on cotton or poly-cotton blends at volumes above 50 units. It delivers the lowest per-unit cost and the most opaque color on dark fabric. Embroidery works best on structured garments like polo shirts, jackets, and hats, but it fails on thin knits (the puckering is permanent). DTG is for full-color photographic art on light-colored garments, small runs only. Heat transfer vinyl (HTV) is for single-color text or simple shapes, but it cracks after 20 washes. Never let a sales rep tell you "we'll just DTG it" for a 200-piece order of black t-shirts, the ink cost kills the margin.

**File formats accepted are narrower than most buyers think.** Vector formats win: Adobe Illustrator (.ai), Encapsulated PostScript (.eps), Scalable Vector Graphics (.svg). These scale infinitely and separate colors cleanly. Raster formats like .png or .jpg are accepted only for DTG, and they must be at least 300 DPI at the final print size. A 72 DPI logo pulled from a website will look blurry at 4 inches wide. The industry standard for embroidery digitizing is .dst or .pes, but you typically pay a separate $15-35 digitizing fee per design. Some platforms include one free digitizing per order, others charge per file.

**E-commerce integration is not plug-and-play.** Shopify, WooCommerce, and BigCommerce all have native connectors for custom apparel platforms, but they sync only product SKUs, not decoration positions or thread colors. You still need a manual approval step for each order's artwork proof. The common workaround: use a product information management (PIM) tool like Plytix or Salsify that stores decoration specs as custom attributes. For B2B portals, most platforms offer a CSV upload that maps customer logos to SKU numbers, but that requires your sales team to standardize logo files first. Expect a 2-4 week integration timeline for anything beyond basic Shopify syncing.

**Pricing structures are deliberately opaque.** Three models dominate. (1) Cost-plus: you pay the garment wholesale price plus decoration fees (screen: $2-4 per location, embroidery: $4-8 per 10,000 stitches). (2) Tiered flat rate: a single price per unit that bundles garment and decoration, with discounts at 50, 100, 250 pieces. (3) Subscription: a monthly retainer (typically $500-2,000) that gives you wholesale pricing without markup, common for corporate uniform programs. The hidden cost is always shipping. A 50-piece order of polo shirts from a US platform to Canada can cost $80-150 in freight alone. Cross-border platforms that consolidate shipments weekly can cut that by 40%.

**Cross-border shipping is where most platforms lose money or customers.** Canada to USA shipments face de minimis thresholds ($800 USD for duty-free commercial imports). USA to Canada is $20 CAD for duty-free, meaning almost every package incurs duties and taxes. The real problem is brokerage fees. FedEx and UPS charge $15-35 per shipment for customs clearance. A platform that uses a customs-bonded warehouse in both countries can avoid per-shipment fees by clearing inventory in bulk once per month. ilogofy, for example, operates in both markets, which lets them treat cross-border orders as domestic fulfillment after a single bulk customs entry. That's rare. Most platforms just pass the brokerage fee to you as a line item.

**Scalability hits a wall around 500 SKUs.** Custom apparel platforms that started as print-on-demand services often break when you need 50 different garment styles, each in 6 colors, with 3 decoration locations. The inventory management system can't handle the combinatorial explosion. The fix: use a platform that treats garments and decorations as separate data objects, not pre-composed products. That way adding a new decoration location doesn't multiply your SKU count. Also, check whether the platform supports "blank inventory" (holding unprinted garments) versus "decorated inventory" (pre-printed). Blank inventory scales linearly with garment types. Decorated inventory scales exponentially and should be avoided for programs over 200 units.

**One honest caveat: no platform handles returns on decorated goods well.** If the embroidery is crooked or the screen print misregistered, the platform will re-make it, but you're out the shipping both ways. For customer-ordered mistakes (wrong size, wrong color), most platforms offer zero recourse. The industry standard is a 3% defect allowance. Anything below that is good. Anything above 5% means the platform's quality control is failing. Ask for their defect rate in writing before you sign a contract.

**Ordering custom patches online is a separate workflow.** Patches require a mold (embroidery) or a screen (woven), which adds $50-150 setup per design. Lead times are 15-20 days because the patch is made on a separate production line, then shipped to the garment decorator for attachment. If you're ordering patches for uniforms, order them 3 weeks before the garments. Most platforms that offer both patch-making and garment decoration will still treat them as two separate orders with two separate shipping charges. ilogofy is one of the few that stitches them together (literally) as a single production step, cutting the total lead time to 12 days.

**Pricing for promotional apparel in Canada is 15-25% higher than US pricing.** Blame the 5% GST/HST, the higher minimum wage in most provinces ($16.55 in Ontario vs $7.25 federal US), and the smaller pool of decorators. If you're a US company buying for a Canadian branch, ask the platform if they have a Canadian production partner. If they don't, you'll pay US labor rates plus cross-border freight plus duties. The math rarely works under 100 pieces.
